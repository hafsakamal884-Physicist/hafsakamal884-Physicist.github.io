About

I'm a physicist with an MSc in Nanotechnology (Ir.) from the University of Twente and a BSc in Physics from PIEAS. My work sits where devices are made and measured.

In the MESA+ cleanroom I have patterned structures with electron-beam lithography, focused ion beam and two-photon lithography, and designed photolithography masks in Clewin. In the lab I run UHV scanning tunnelling microscopy and spectroscopy, AFM and optical inspection, and I write my own Python tools to analyse the data.

Most recently I interned at TKF Subsea Solutions, working on a partial discharge measurement setup for high-voltage cable systems. I'm now looking for engineering roles in semiconductor and MEMS fabrication, metrology, failure analysis and equipment engineering in the Netherlands and Belgium.

My favourite part of lab work is when an instrument isn't behaving, and someone has to work out whether it's the sample, the tip, the vacuum or the electronics.

MSc thesis, University of Twente, 2025 to 2026

Bismuth on silicon carbide, measured atom by atom

In the Physics of Interfaces and Nanomaterials group, I studied ultrathin bismuth on epitaxial graphene on 6H-SiC(0001) with room-temperature UHV scanning tunnelling microscopy and spectroscopy.

The work combined sample preparation and thermal evaporation in ultra-high vacuum, tip preparation, systematic imaging and I–V spectroscopy, and a lot of diagnosis: separating real structure from tip artefacts, drift and noise.

Software, MSc thesis, 2025 to 2026

Python tool for automated STM image analysis

Measuring nanoribbon orientations by hand is slow and hard to reproduce. I wrote a Python and Matplotlib tool that extracts ribbon orientation angles from STM images, plots their distribution and analyses their symmetry.

Every image in a dataset is now processed the same way, which makes results repeatable and comparable. It's the kind of automation production metrology depends on.

Industry internship, TKF Subsea Solutions, Lochem, April to June 2026

Partial discharge measurement for high-voltage cables

At TKF I worked on a local partial discharge setup for high-voltage subsea cable systems. Partial discharges are tiny pulses buried in electrical noise, so the work is metrology at heart: a reliable measurement chain, separating real events from interference, and repeatable results.

Part of that is knowing what isn't a discharge. Sharp pulses that repeat at fixed points on the voltage cycle are switching noise from the source, and they inflate the reading until gating removes them. A different scale from STM, with the same question underneath: is it the device, or the measurement?

  • High voltage
  • Partial discharge
  • PRPD analysis
  • Noise and interference
  • IEC 60270

BSc thesis, PIEAS, 2024

Band structure of twisted bilayer graphene

I implemented the Bistritzer–MacDonald continuum model in Python and reproduced the moiré band structures across twist angles, including the flat bands near the 1.05° magic angle.

It was my first project turning a published model into working code and validating it against known results.

Model after R. Bistritzer and A. H. MacDonald, PNAS 108(30), 12233–12237 (2011).
